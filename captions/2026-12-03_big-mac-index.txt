The same Big Mac costs $9.08 in Zurich and $2.47 in Taipei 🍔

The Economist has published the Big Mac index since 1986. The idea is simple: an identical product, made the same way in 100+ countries, should cost about the same once you convert currencies. It doesn't — and the gap tells you which currencies are cheap and which are expensive.

January 2026 edition: Switzerland $9.08, United States $6.12, Taiwan $2.47. On that measure the Swiss franc looks roughly 48% overvalued against the dollar, the Taiwan dollar roughly 60% undervalued 🌍

It's a joke that turned out to be useful. It's also the most intuitive explanation of why your holiday felt expensive in one country and absurdly cheap in another — your money didn't change, its purchasing power across borders did.

(It's a rough gauge: local wages, rents and taxes all push the price around. Nobody trades on burgers.)

Source: The Economist Big Mac index, January 2026.
