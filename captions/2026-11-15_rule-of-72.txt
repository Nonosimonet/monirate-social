The only investing maths you need to memorise: 72 ÷ rate ⏱️

Divide 72 by your annual return and you get, near enough, the number of years for your money to double.

▪️ 8% a year → 9 years
▪️ 4.2% in a top savings account → 17 years
▪️ 0.37%, the US national average savings rate → 195 years 💀

Same money, same you, same effort. The only variable is the rate — and the difference between "my bank" and "a bank" is roughly 178 years of waiting.

The rule is an approximation (it's most accurate between about 6% and 10%), but it's good enough to do in your head, in a shop, while someone sells you something.

Two honest caveats: 8% is a long-run average, not a yearly guarantee, and inflation eats into both sides — at 3% inflation, money doubling in 9 years only really doubles in about 24.

Source: FDIC national average savings rate, September 2026 (0.37%).
