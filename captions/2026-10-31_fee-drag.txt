1% a year costs you 25% of your retirement 🧾

$10,000 invested for 30 years:
▪️ at 7% a year → $76,123
▪️ at 6% a year (same thing, minus a 1% fee) → $57,435

The fee is 1%. The damage is 25% 😳

It works that way because a fee doesn't just take this year's one percent — it takes everything that one percent would have earned for the next three decades. Compounding runs in both directions, and the fee has a thirty-year head start on you.

Where to look: fund expense ratios (an index tracker can be under 0.10%, some active funds are above 1.5%), advisory fees, platform fees, and the currency spread your broker applies when you buy foreign assets — that last one hides in the price, not the invoice.

Figures are a straight compounding calculation at a constant rate, not a market forecast.
