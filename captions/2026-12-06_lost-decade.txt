Ten years in the S&P 500 and you ended with less than you started 😶

$10,000 invested at the start of 2000, dividends reinvested, was worth about $9,090 at the end of 2009. The "lost decade": roughly -0.95% a year for ten years.

Then 2010 to 2019 returned about +257%.

Same index. Same strategy. Two decades that would teach you completely opposite lessons about investing 🎭

This is what "stocks return 10% a year" hides. The average is real over long periods, but nobody lives in the average — you live in whichever decade you happened to be saving through, and the starting valuation matters enormously. The 2000s began with the S&P trading above 29x earnings.

Two takeaways, neither comfortable: anyone promising you a decade of returns is guessing, and the people who kept buying through 2000-2009 are precisely the ones the 2010s made rich.

Sources: S&P 500 total return, 1999 year-end to 2009 year-end; annual total returns 2010-2019.
