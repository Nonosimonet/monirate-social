"I'll invest after the pullback." The data says don't 📈

J.P. Morgan looked at every S&P 500 record high from 1988 to 2025. Investing on a day the index closed at an all-time high returned about 82% over the following five years. Investing on a random day: about 76%.

Buying the record did better. Not by a lot — but it didn't punish you, which is the opposite of what the fear predicts.

Two more from the same research: 81% of all-time highs were followed by another all-time high within a week, and RBC found that over any five-year period after a record, the index has never finished down more than 10% 😮

The honest caveat: Citi ran a 65-year version and found the difference is close to zero. Nobody finds that buying highs hurts — the debate is only about how small the edge is.

The S&P closed at a record on October 6, 2026. That's not a reason to buy. It's also not a reason to wait.

Sources: J.P. Morgan Guide to the Markets; RBC GAM; Citi.
