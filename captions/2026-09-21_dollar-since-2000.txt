Your $100 bill from 2000 is now a $52 bill 💸

US consumer prices rose about 94% between 2000 and 2026 (CPI 172.2 → 333.9). What $100 bought in 2000 costs $194 today.

Flip it around and it's brutal: a $100 note left in a drawer since 2000 buys what $51.57 buys now. It lost 48% of its purchasing power without moving 😐

Nobody sends you a statement for this. There's no red number, no bad trade, no notification. The bill still says one hundred.

That's the real reason "doing nothing with cash" isn't the safe option people think it is — it's a slow, silent, guaranteed loss. The only question is what you accept as the alternative risk.

Source: US Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI-U, 2000 vs 2026.
