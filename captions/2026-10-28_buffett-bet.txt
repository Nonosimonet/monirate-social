Buffett bet $1,000,000 that doing nothing would win. It did 🏆

The bet ran from January 2008 to December 2017. Buffett picked a Vanguard S&P 500 index fund. Ted Seides picked five funds-of-funds, run by professionals who could go anywhere, short anything, and charge for the privilege.

Final score: the index fund +125.8%. The five funds returned 21.7%, 42.3%, 87.7%, 2.8% and 27.0% — an average of about 36% 📊

Not one of the five beat the index. Seides conceded before the clock ran out.

What actually did the work: no manager fees, no performance fees, no trading, no clever calls. The index simply kept the returns instead of paying them away. Over ten years that difference compounded into a gap nobody could close.

The lesson isn't that professionals are stupid. It's that costs and activity are a tax you pay with certainty, while outperformance is a hope you pay for in advance.

Source: the Buffett–Protégé Partners wager, 2008–2017 results.
