In 1971 an ounce of gold cost $35. Today: $4,195 🪙

On August 15, 1971, Nixon ended the dollar's convertibility into gold. The price had been fixed by law at $35 an ounce. Fifty-five years later it's about $4,195 — roughly 120 times more.

Meanwhile, $1 from 1971 has the purchasing power of about $8.15 today. Read it the other way: a 1971 dollar buys about 12 cents' worth of goods now. The dollar lost roughly 88% of its buying power 💸

Gold didn't get heavier, rarer or more useful. The unit it's priced in got smaller.

That's the honest frame for every long-horizon number you'll ever see — your salary, your house, your portfolio. A price going up and money going down look identical on a chart, and they are not the same thing.

The counterpoint worth keeping: over those same 55 years, US stocks beat gold comfortably. Protecting against debasement and growing wealth are two different jobs.

Sources: BLS CPI 1971→2026; gold spot October 2026.
